Axisymmetric bubbles

We consider the local bubble and bubble ring families rigorously constructed in {{cite:mns}} and {{cite:ms}} and continue them numerically. It turns out that they are connected. The near-spherical bubbles flatten, pass a largest Weber number and end when the two poles touch. The bubble rings start from that same touching bubble and, as their hole opens, become the thin rings of {{cite:ms}}. To prove this connection rigorously remains an interesting open problem.

The problem

We consider steady axisymmetric solutions of the Euler equations with surface tension, where by the Young–Laplace law the pressure jumps across the interface by \(\sigma H\), with \(\sigma\) the surface tension and \(H\) the mean curvature. A bubble \(B\), filled with vacuum, moves with constant velocity \(V e_z\) through an inviscid, incompressible fluid of density \(\rho\) that is at rest at infinity. In the frame moving with the bubble the flow is steady and, outside the bubble, irrotational: \(u = \nabla\varphi\). After rescaling lengths by the radius \(R\) of the ball with the same volume and velocities by \(V\), a single parameter remains, the Weber number

\[ \mathrm{We} = \frac{\rho V^2 R}{\sigma}. \]

Bernoulli's law together with the Young–Laplace law turns the problem into an overdetermined free boundary value problem for the bubble \(B\) and the velocity potential \(\varphi\):

\[ \begin{cases} \Delta \varphi = 0 & \text{in } \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \overline{B}, \\[2pt] \partial_n \varphi = 0 & \text{on } \partial B, \\[2pt] \nabla \varphi \to -e_z & \text{as } |x| \to \infty, \\[2pt] H - \dfrac{\mathrm{We}}{2}\, |\nabla \varphi|^2 = \lambda & \text{on } \partial B, \\[2pt] |B| = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}. \end{cases} \]

Here \(H\) is the sum of the principal curvatures of \(\partial B\) (\(H = 2\) on the unit sphere), and \(\lambda\) is an unknown constant. For a given \(B\) the first three equations determine \(\varphi\) and the fourth is the extra condition that selects the shape. We look for \(B\) axisymmetric about the \(z\)-axis and symmetric under \(z \mapsto -z\). This is the hollow-vortex case of the problem studied in {{cite:mns}}. When \(B\) is a solid torus, a bubble ring, the flow may circulate around the tube, as in {{cite:ms}}; the rings then form a two-parameter family, and we follow the rings with zero net flux through the hole.

Method

Shapes. The meridian of a bubble is a curve in the half plane \(r \ge 0\). We describe it by its tangent angle, expanded in a Fourier sine series whose symmetries encode regularity on the axis and the reflection \(z \mapsto -z\); the curve then closes by construction. The cross-section of a ring is described in the same way, with a parametrisation that concentrates points in its narrow neck near the axis.

Flow. For a bubble, \(\varphi\) solves an exterior Neumann problem. We write it as a boundary integral equation on the meridian, with the kernels integrated over the azimuthal angle in closed form (complete elliptic integrals), and solve it by a high-order Nyström method with corrections for the singular and nearly singular integrals. For a ring we represent the flow by a vortex sheet on its surface; the sheet strength follows from the condition of zero flux through the hole, and the circulation \(\Gamma\) from the sheet strength.

Continuation. The boundary condition \(H - \tfrac{\mathrm{We}}{2}|\nabla\varphi|^2 = \lambda\), projected onto Fourier modes, is solved together with the volume constraint by Newton's method, with \(\lambda\) and the shape as unknowns. Pseudo-arclength continuation carries the bubbles from the sphere through the fold; the pole height and then the hole radius serve as parameters towards and beyond the touching bubble. The resolution is increased along the way, every value is computed at several resolutions, and a second, independent solver (a different shape representation and flow solver) reproduces the largest Weber number to 13 digits.

Bifurcations. The steady states are the critical points of the energy \(|\partial B| - \tfrac{\mathrm{We}}{2}\, m_{zz}\) at fixed volume, where \(m_{zz}\) is the added mass. Along the whole branch we compute the eigenvalues of its second variation, separately for perturbations with azimuthal wave number \(m\) that are even or odd under \(z \mapsto -z\). A branch can bifurcate only where an eigenvalue crosses zero. Within the axisymmetric class (\(m = 0\)) we found no bifurcation: the eigenvalues change sign only at the two folds, the largest Weber number of the bubbles and that of the rings. In particular no pear-shaped bubbles branch off. For the thinnest rings, with hole radius above about 340, the smallest eigenvalue drops below the accuracy of the computation.

Other bifurcations. Along the bubble branch we found no crossing for any \(m\), so no non-axisymmetric bubble branches off it. The rings are different. At hole radius 0.078 a branch with an off-centre hole (\(m = 1\)) branches off, and from hole radius 0.67 on, one beaded branch for each \(m = 2, 3, \dots\), at the hole radii where a hollow tube with swirl becomes unstable to varicose (Rayleigh–Plateau) perturbations {{cite:ponstein}}; there are more and more of them as the rings grow. These are the diamonds in the bifurcation diagram. If the bubble may also rotate about its direction of motion, bubbles moving on helical paths appear {{cite:davinci}}.

Values excluded in {{cite:ms}}. The construction of thin rings in {{cite:ms}} covers every limiting value of the circulation Weber number \(\rho\Gamma^2/(4\pi^2\bar\varepsilon\sigma)\), with \(\bar\varepsilon\) the radius of the cross-section, except \(3, 4, 5, \dots\). At these values the linearised problem degenerates, and {{cite:ms}} expect rings with a non-circular cross-section to branch off, as for two-dimensional bubbles with circulation, the limit of the cross-section: there the circle bifurcates at these values {{cite:wegmanncrowdy}}, and it is the only solution below the first one, 3 {{cite:niebel2d}}. These would be axisymmetric branches. On the zero-flux rings this Weber number is at most 1.1, near the touching bubble, and below 0.3 once the rings are thin, so the excluded values are never reached; they belong to rings with a strong backflow through the hole, which we do not follow here.
